## Bottom line

The sharp edge is not agents editing `MEMORY.md`. It is **evaluator-gated search over code, experiments, and training systems**: propose a change, run an objective evaluation, retain winners, repeat. That is demonstrated in constrained domains. What is not demonstrated publicly is an autonomous system that chooses important AI research problems, reliably runs the full research program for weeks, changes its own training process, and compounds capability without humans setting goals and validating outputs. Big difference, tiny word “just”—humans do love hiding cliffs behind it.

“Measured evidence” below means an actual reported experiment, deployment, benchmark, or tracked internal metric; it does not necessarily mean independently replicated.

## Serious skeptical / limiting signals

- METR warns that its horizon is “**not the length of time AIs can work independently**”; it is a 50%-success estimate for replacement of serial human labor on its suite. It also says horizon error bars have been roughly a factor of two, vary by orders of magnitude across domains, and may be distorted by benchmark construction. [METR’s limitations note](https://metr.substack.com/p/2026-01-22-time-horizon-limitations)

- Anthropic’s own R&D evaluation is the most important counterweight to frontier rhetoric: “**None believed the model could replace an entry-level researcher within three months**.” It reports struggles with longer tasks, new information, and large codebases. [Anthropic Transparency Hub](https://www.anthropic.com/transparency)

- OpenAI says the least automatable tasks become the bottlenecks as automation rises, and that agent-powered AI research is “**still new**” with hard-to-validate metrics. [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/research-acceleration-view-inside-openai/)

- Anthropic’s RSI discussion explicitly admits an alternative: capability curves could bend into S-curves; research judgment may require a new architectural idea; energy, chip fabrication, or interconnect could bind progress. [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/institute/recursive-self-improvement)

## State of the frontier

**Demonstrated**

- Closed-loop improvement where progress is cheaply and objectively scored: algorithms, kernels, code patches, Kaggle-style ML work, and constrained experiment optimization.
- Large productivity amplification for researchers using many agents.
- Agents can improve their surrounding scaffold, search strategy, tool use, and candidate programs when a benchmark/evaluator selects winners.

**Claimed**

- That these loops will transfer from evaluator-friendly optimization to broad AI research.
- That agent labor can soon automate whole elite research teams.
- That automation of AI research will yield a self-reinforcing, potentially explosive capability loop.

**Conspicuously missing**

- A public, independently verified, end-to-end autonomous recursive loop that repeatedly improves a frontier model or its training recipe over multiple generations.
- Robust multi-week research autonomy: problem selection, experiment design, debugging, interpretation, prioritization, collaboration, and trustworthy integration.
- Evidence that the hardest bottlenecks—research taste, evaluation design, data/compute acquisition, hardware, governance, and validation—are themselves automated.
- Reliable proof that gains on self-modification or benchmark optimization translate into broad, compounding general intelligence.
